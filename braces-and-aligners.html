Braces & Aligners

Gently guiding your teeth into perfect alignment — with modern orthodontic solutions tailored to your needs, age, and lifestyle.

Overview

What Are Braces & Aligners?

Braces and aligners are orthodontic devices that straighten and align teeth by applying consistent, controlled pressure over time. Both are highly effective — the best choice depends on your specific needs, lifestyle, and the complexity of your case.

Beyond aesthetics, properly aligned teeth are easier to clean, reduce the risk of gum disease, distribute bite forces more evenly, and can eliminate jaw-related pain — making orthodontic treatment a long-term investment in your oral health, not just your appearance.

Braces or Aligners — Which Is Right for You?

Both are proven orthodontic solutions. Your clinician will help determine the best fit based on your case complexity, age, and personal preference.

B
Traditional Braces

Braces use metal or ceramic brackets bonded to the surface of each tooth, connected by a wire that is periodically tightened. They are fixed, meaning they work continuously without relying on patient compliance. Braces are particularly effective for complex cases involving rotations, vertical movement, or significant bite correction.

A
Clear Aligners

Clear aligners are a series of custom-fitted, transparent trays that are replaced every 1–2 weeks as your teeth gradually shift. They are virtually invisible, removable for eating and brushing, and generally more comfortable than fixed braces. They are ideal for mild to moderate alignment issues and are popular with adult patients seeking a discreet treatment option.

Indications

When You May Need Braces or Aligners

Crooked Teeth

Crooked Teeth

Crowded or crooked teeth are one of the most common reasons to pursue orthodontic treatment — both for aesthetics and ease of cleaning.

Overbite or Underbite

Overbite or Underbite

When the upper or lower teeth extend too far beyond the opposite set, it creates bite imbalance that can cause wear and jaw strain.

Crossbite

Crossbite

When upper and lower jaws do not meet correctly, braces or aligners can bring them into proper functional alignment.

Jaw Problems

Jaw Problems

Misaligned jaws can cause chronic pain, difficulty chewing, or clicking sounds. Orthodontic correction can relieve these symptoms.

Aesthetic Goals

Aesthetic Goals

Many patients choose orthodontic treatment simply for a more symmetrical, attractive smile — a completely valid and worthwhile reason.

Speech Difficulties

Speech Difficulties

Misaligned teeth or jaws can affect pronunciation and speech clarity. Correcting alignment often substantially improves articulation.

Your Treatment Journey

1
Assessment & Records

A comprehensive orthodontic evaluation including X-rays, photographs, and digital scans is taken to fully map your bite, tooth positions, and jaw relationship. This forms the basis of your customised treatment plan.

2
Appliance Fitting

For braces, brackets are bonded to the teeth and a wire is placed. For aligners, your custom-fabricated first set of trays is delivered and fitted. We walk you through everything you need to know about wearing and caring for your new appliance.

3
Regular Reviews & Adjustments

Braces require periodic wire adjustments every 4–8 weeks. Aligner patients collect new trays and check in regularly to assess progress. At each appointment we monitor movement and make any necessary refinements to keep your treatment on track.

4
Retention

Once your teeth have moved into their final positions, the active appliance is removed and retainers are provided. Retainers hold your result stable while the bone fully settles around the repositioned teeth — an essential final phase that protects everything you have achieved.

Aftercare

Post-Treatment Care

Good habits after orthodontic treatment protect your result and keep your smile straight for life.

  • Wear your retainer as directed: This is non-negotiable. Teeth will shift without retention — wearing your retainer consistently is what preserves the entire outcome of your treatment.
  • Brush and floss daily: Use fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth every day to prevent cavities and gum disease, which can emerge unbothered by the protection of braces.
  • Continue regular dental check-ups: Visit us every six months for professional cleaning and monitoring — especially important in the first year after treatment.
  • Avoid hard, sticky foods: These can damage teeth and retainers. Even after braces are off, protect the health of your newly aligned teeth.
  • Care for a permanent retainer: If fitted with a bonded wire retainer, use a floss threader or interdental brush to thoroughly clean beneath it daily.
  • Care for a removable retainer: Clean with a soft toothbrush and mild soap or a retainer-cleaning solution. Do not use toothpaste, which can scratch clear retainers.
  • Report any discomfort: If your retainer feels tight after a break in wearing, or if you have any pain or concerns, contact us promptly rather than waiting for your next appointment.
