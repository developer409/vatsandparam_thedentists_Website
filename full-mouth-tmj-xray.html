Full Mouth X-Ray & TMJ X-Ray

Precision diagnostics for a complete understanding of your oral health — using advanced panoramic and specialized joint imaging to identify issues at their source.

Panoramic Imaging

What is a Full Mouth X-Ray?

A full mouth X-ray, also known as a panoramic X-ray or orthopantomogram (OPG), is a dental radiograph that provides a comprehensive view of a patient's teeth, jaws, and surrounding structures.

It is an essential tool to diagnose and treat dental problems, such as cavities, gum disease, impacted teeth, and jaw disorders. By capturing the entire mouth in a single image, it allows for a holistic assessment of your dental health.

The Imaging Process

The process involves the patient standing or sitting still while a machine rotates around their head, capturing images of the upper and lower jaws, teeth, and surrounding bone structures. The image produced is a flat, two-dimensional view that displays the entire mouth in one shot.

Who Needs It?

Full mouth X-rays are particularly useful for patients with complex dental problems or for those undergoing extensive work, such as full-mouth restorations or orthodontic treatment. They are also used to assess growth in children and detect abnormalities in the jawbone.

Specialized Diagnostics

About TMJ X-Ray

Precision Joint Imaging

A TMJ (temporomandibular joint) X-ray, also known as a TMJ radiograph, is a specialized dental X-ray that provides a detailed view of the temporomandibular joint. This joint connects the jawbone to the skull and is responsible for movements like speaking, chewing, and opening the mouth.

TMJ X-rays are used to diagnose and evaluate the severity of TMJ disorders, which can cause pain, clicking, popping, and limited movement in the jaw.

The Procedure

The procedure involves the patient opening their mouth wide and positioning their head in a specific way to capture images of the joint. The images are typically taken in both the open and closed positions to assess the joint's movement and function.
