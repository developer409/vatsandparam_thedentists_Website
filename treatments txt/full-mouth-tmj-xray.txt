Full Mouth X-Ray & TMJ X-Ray 
Precision diagnostics for a complete understanding of your oral health — using advanced panoramic and specialized joint imaging to identify issues at their source. 

Panoramic Imaging 
What is a Full Mouth X-Ray? 

A full mouth X-ray, also known as a panoramic X-ray or orthopantomogram (OPG) , is a dental radiograph that provides a comprehensive view of a patient's teeth, jaws, and surrounding structures. 
It is an essential tool to diagnose and treat dental problems, such as cavities, gum disease, impacted teeth, and jaw disorders. By capturing the entire mouth in a single image, it allows for a holistic assessment of your dental health. 

The Imaging Process 
The process involves the patient standing or sitting still while a machine rotates around their head, capturing images of the upper and lower jaws, teeth, and surrounding bone structures. The image produced is a flat, two-dimensional view that displays the entire mouth in one shot. 

Who Needs It? 
Full mouth X-rays are particularly useful for patients with complex dental problems or for those undergoing extensive work, such as full-mouth restorations or orthodontic treatment . They are also used to assess growth in children and detect abnormalities in the jawbone. 

Specialized Diagnostics 
About TMJ X-Ray 

Precision Joint Imaging 
A TMJ (temporomandibular joint) X-ray, also known as a TMJ radiograph, is a specialized dental X-ray that provides a detailed view of the temporomandibular joint. This joint connects the jawbone to the skull and is responsible for movements like speaking, chewing, and opening the mouth. 
TMJ X-rays are used to diagnose and evaluate the severity of TMJ disorders, which can cause pain, clicking, popping, and limited movement in the jaw. 

The Procedure 
The procedure involves the patient opening their mouth wide and positioning their head in a specific way to capture images of the joint. The images are typically taken in both the open and closed positions to assess the joint's movement and function. 

Frequently Asked Questions 

Full Mouth X-Ray FAQs 

Why do I need a full mouth X-ray? + 

It provides a comprehensive view of your entire oral cavity, including teeth, jaws, and bone structure, allowing dentists to detect issues like impacted teeth, cysts, tumors, and bone loss that standard X-rays might miss. 

Is a full mouth X-ray safe? + 

Yes, modern digital panoramic X-rays use extremely low levels of radiation. We follow all safety protocols to ensure minimal exposure. 

How long does it take to get a full mouth X-ray? + 

The actual imaging process takes only about 10–20 seconds. The entire appointment, including preparation, usually lasts under 10 minutes. 

Is there any special preparation required? + 

No special preparation is needed. However, you will be asked to remove any metal objects such as jewelry, eyeglasses, or hairpins from the head and neck area as they can interfere with the image. 

How often should I get a full mouth X-ray? + 

The frequency depends on your specific oral health needs and treatment plan. Generally, they are taken once every 3–5 years for screening, or more frequently for active orthodontic or implant cases. 

TMJ X-Ray FAQs 

What is a TMJ X-ray? + 

A TMJ X-ray is a specialized radiograph that focuses specifically on the joint connecting your jaw to your skull, helping to visualize its position, condition, and movement. 

Why do I need a TMJ X-ray? + 

It is clinically indicated if you experience symptoms like chronic jaw pain, clicking or popping sounds while chewing, or difficulty opening your mouth wide. 

Is a TMJ X-ray safe? + 

Yes, much like panoramic X-rays, TMJ radiographs use minimal digital radiation and are a safe diagnostic standard in dentistry. 

How long does it take to get a TMJ X-ray? + 

The procedure is quick, typically taking less than 15 minutes to capture images in both the open and closed jaw positions. 

Precision Diagnostics for Better Outcomes 
Both full mouth and TMJ X-rays are vital tools that allow us to move beyond symptoms and treat the root cause of dental problems. They are safe, effective, and essential for evaluating your complete dental health.
